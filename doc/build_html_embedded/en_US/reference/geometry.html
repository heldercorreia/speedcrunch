Geometry

Use area_ for flat shapes and for the total surface area of solids. Use volume_ for volume. The _lateral suffix gives the side or curved area. For a composite solid, add the volumes of its non-overlapping parts and count only its exposed faces for surface area.

Enter measurements as plain numbers in a common unit, or give each measurement a unit. Physical lengths can use different units, such as meters and centimeters. Results have square or cubic units when the inputs carry units. Angles without units follow the current angle mode. An explicit unit such as [deg] works in any mode. Heights are perpendicular to the base. Use positive lengths and areas unless the function gives a range that includes zero or negative values.

For shapes given by coordinates, enter a matrix with one point or vector per row. The optional scale is the length of one coordinate unit, for example 1[cm]. With no scale, the result is a plain number.

Exact results

With any decimal notation, SpeedCrunch adds a line below the decimal result when it finds a simple exact form for a Geometry calculation. Rational uses that form on its result line. This also works in arithmetic such as 2 * area_circle(2).

The display recognizes fractions such as 1 / 6, multiples of π or π², square roots, and some sums and differences. The exact form can help you check your work against a formula or reuse the value without rounding. For example, in area_rectangular_pyramid_lateral(8; 6; 4), one pair of side faces contributes 40 and the other contributes 24 · √(2).

SpeedCrunch checks the calculated value before rounding it for display. For example, a sector of 90 degrees with radius 1 matches π / 4. A sector just under 90 degrees can round to the same decimal and show only the decimal line.

Plane shapes

area_square(side)

Square the side length.

Example:

area_square(4)
= 16
area_rectangle(length; width)

Multiply the length by the width.

Example:

area_rectangle(4; 3)
= 12
area_triangle(base; height)

Multiply the base by the perpendicular height and divide by 2. For a right triangle, use the two legs.

Example:

area_triangle(4; 3)
= 6
area_triangle_from_sides(a; b; c)

Use Heron’s formula, √(s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)), where s = (a + b + c) / 2. The sides must form a triangle.

Example:

area_triangle_from_sides(3; 4; 5)
= 6
area_triangle_from_angle(a; b; angle)

Use ab sin(θ) / 2, where θ is the angle between the two sides. It must be between 0 and 180 degrees.

Example:

area_triangle_from_angle(3; 4; 90[deg])
= 6
area_parallelogram(base; height)

Multiply the base by the perpendicular height.

Example:

area_parallelogram(5; 3)
= 15
area_trapezoid(base_1; base_2; height)

Average the lengths of the parallel bases and multiply by the perpendicular height. This shape is also called a trapezium.

Example:

area_trapezoid(3; 7; 2)
= 10
area_rhombus(diagonal_1; diagonal_2)

Multiply the diagonal lengths and divide by 2.

Example:

area_rhombus(4; 6)
= 12
area_kite(diagonal_1; diagonal_2)

Multiply the diagonal lengths and divide by 2.

Example:

area_kite(5; 8)
= 20
area_regular_polygon(n; side)

Use n s² / (4 tan(π / n)) for side length s and a whole number of sides n ≥ 3. This covers equilateral triangles and regular polygons of any size.

Example:

area_regular_polygon(4; 3)
= 9
area_polygon(vertices[; scale])

Use the shoelace formula. For each edge from (x₁, y₁) to (x₂, y₂), compute x₁y₂ − y₁x₂. Add these values, take the absolute value, and divide by 2. Enter each vertex as an {x; y} row in boundary order, clockwise or counterclockwise. The last vertex connects to the first. Edges may meet at their shared endpoints. You can repeat the first vertex at the end.

Example:

area_polygon({{0; 0}; {4; 0}; {4; 3}; {0; 3}}; 1[m])
= 12 m²
area_circle(radius)

Use πr². For a diameter, pass half of it as the radius.

Example:

area_circle(2)
= 4pi
area_circular_sector(radius; angle)

Use r²θ / 2, with the sweep angle θ converted to radians. Use 90 degrees for a quarter circle and 180 degrees for a semicircle. The sweep can range from 0 to 360 degrees.

Example:

area_circular_sector(2; 90[deg])
= pi
area_circular_segment(radius; depth)

Measure the depth from the chord to the arc along the perpendicular through the center. Depth ranges from zero to the diameter. Use the segment formula below with θ = 4 arcsin(√(depth / (2r))).

Example:

area_circular_segment(2; 2)
= 2pi
area_circular_segment_from_angle(radius; angle)

Use r²(θ − sin(θ)) / 2, with the central angle θ converted to radians. The angle can range from 0 to 360 degrees.

Example:

area_circular_segment_from_angle(2; 180[deg])
= 2pi
area_ellipse(semi_axis_1; semi_axis_2)

Multiply the two semiaxes by π. A semiaxis is half of the full axis length.

Example:

area_ellipse(3; 2)
= 6pi
area_annulus(outer_radius; inner_radius)

Use π(R² − r²) for a ring with inner radius 0 ≤ r ≤ R.

Example:

area_annulus(3; 2)
= 5pi
area_annular_sector(outer_radius; inner_radius; angle)

Use (R² − r²)θ / 2, with the sweep converted to radians. The inner radius can range from zero to the outer radius. The sweep can range from 0 to 360 degrees.

Example:

area_annular_sector(3; 2; 180[deg])
= 2.5pi
area_rounded_rectangle(length; width; corner_radius)

Use lw − (4 − π)r² for four equally rounded corners. The corner radius is at most half the shorter side.

Example:

area_rounded_rectangle(4; 2; 1)
= 4 + pi
area_stadium(straight_length; radius)

Add the area of the rectangular middle to that of the two semicircles, 2rL + π r². The straight length L is the distance between the arc centers. This shape is also called an obround.

Example:

area_stadium(3; 1)
= 6 + pi
area_parabolic_segment(chord; rise)

Use 2bh / 3 for a chord of length b perpendicular to the symmetry axis. Measure the rise h from the chord to the vertex. A rise of zero gives zero area.

Example:

area_parabolic_segment(6; 2)
= 8
area_circle_overlap(radius_1; radius_2; center_distance)

Measure the separation between the centers. The overlap is the sum of two circular segments in the partial case. Circles that only touch have zero overlap. If one circle contains the other, use the smaller circle’s area.

Example:

area_circle_overlap(2; 1; 1)
= pi

Prisms and pyramids

area_cube(edge)

Add the areas of all six square faces, 6s².

Example:

area_cube(2)
= 24
area_cube_lateral(edge)

Add the areas of the four side faces, 4s².

Example:

area_cube_lateral(2)
= 16
volume_cube(edge)

Cube the edge length.

Example:

volume_cube(3)
= 27
area_cuboid(length; width; height)

Use 2(lw + lh + wh) for a rectangular prism.

Example:

area_cuboid(4; 3; 2)
= 52
area_cuboid_lateral(length; width; height)

Use 2(l + w)h for the four side faces around the length and width of the base.

Example:

area_cuboid_lateral(4; 3; 2)
= 28
volume_cuboid(length; width; height)

Multiply length, width, and height.

Example:

volume_cuboid(4; 3; 2)
= 24
area_prism(base_area; base_perimeter; height)

Use 2A + Ph, where A and P are the area and perimeter of one base.

Example:

area_prism(6; 12; 4)
= 60
area_prism_lateral(base_perimeter; height)

Multiply the base perimeter by the height.

Example:

area_prism_lateral(12; 4)
= 48
volume_prism(base_area; height)

Multiply the base area by the perpendicular height. This also works for oblique prisms.

Example:

volume_prism(6; 4)
= 24
area_triangular_prism(a; b; c; height)

Find the area of the triangular base from its three sides and use area_prism(). The height is the distance between the triangular faces.

Example:

area_triangular_prism(3; 4; 5; 4)
= 60
area_triangular_prism_lateral(a; b; c; height)

Multiply the sum of the three base sides by the prism’s height.

Example:

area_triangular_prism_lateral(3; 4; 5; 4)
= 48
volume_triangular_prism(a; b; c; height)

Multiply the base area from Heron’s formula by the prism’s height.

Example:

volume_triangular_prism(3; 4; 5; 4)
= 24
area_regular_prism(n; side; height)

Add both regular polygon bases and the sides. Use area_regular_polygon() for the base area.

Example:

area_regular_prism(4; 3; 2)
= 42
area_regular_prism_lateral(n; side; height)

Multiply the height by the regular polygon’s perimeter ns.

Example:

area_regular_prism_lateral(4; 3; 2)
= 24
volume_regular_prism(n; side; height)

Multiply the area of the regular polygon base by the height.

Example:

volume_regular_prism(4; 3; 2)
= 18
volume_pyramid(base_area; height)

Multiply the base area by the perpendicular height and divide by 3.

Example:

volume_pyramid(16; 6)
= 32
area_square_pyramid(side; height)

Add the square base to the four triangular sides. The apex is above the center of the base.

Example:

area_square_pyramid(6; 4)
= 96
area_square_pyramid_lateral(side; height)

Use 2s√(h² + (s / 2)²) for the four triangular sides.

Example:

area_square_pyramid_lateral(6; 4)
= 60
volume_square_pyramid(side; height)

Use s²h / 3.

Example:

volume_square_pyramid(6; 4)
= 48
area_rectangular_pyramid(length; width; height)

Add the rectangular base to the four triangular sides. The apex is above the center of the base.

Example:

area_rectangular_pyramid(6; 6; 4)
= 96
area_rectangular_pyramid_lateral(length; width; height)

Use l√(h² + (w / 2)²) + w√(h² + (l / 2)²). Each pair of opposite faces shares a slant height.

Example:

area_rectangular_pyramid_lateral(8; 6; 4)
= 73.94112549695428117124
= 40 + 24 · √(2)
volume_rectangular_pyramid(length; width; height)

Use lwh / 3.

Example:

volume_rectangular_pyramid(8; 6; 4)
= 64
area_regular_pyramid(n; side; height)

Add the regular polygon base and the lateral area. The apex is above the center of the base.

Example:

area_regular_pyramid(4; 6; 4)
= 96
area_regular_pyramid_lateral(n; side; height)

Multiply half the base perimeter by √(h² + a²), where a is the distance from the base center to a side.

Example:

area_regular_pyramid_lateral(4; 6; 4)
= 60
volume_regular_pyramid(n; side; height)

Multiply the regular polygon’s area by the height and divide by 3.

Example:

volume_regular_pyramid(4; 6; 4)
= 48
area_regular_pyramid_frustum(n; side_1; side_2; height)

Add both regular polygon bases and the lateral area between them. A side length of zero gives a pyramid.

Example:

area_regular_pyramid_frustum(4; 6; 0; 4)
= 96
area_regular_pyramid_frustum_lateral(n; side_1; side_2; height)

Average the two base perimeters and multiply by the slant height. The slant height is computed from the perpendicular height and the difference between the two base apothems.

Example:

area_regular_pyramid_frustum_lateral(4; 6; 6; 4)
= 96
volume_regular_pyramid_frustum(n; side_1; side_2; height)

Compute the two regular polygon base areas and use volume_pyramid_frustum(). At least one side length must be positive.

Example:

volume_regular_pyramid_frustum(4; 6; 3; 4)
= 84
volume_pyramid_frustum(base_area_1; base_area_2; height)

Use h(A₁ + A₂ + √(A₁ A₂)) / 3 for two parallel cuts of a pyramid. A base area of zero gives the tip.

Example:

volume_pyramid_frustum(36; 9; 4)
= 84
volume_prismoid(end_area_1; mid_area; end_area_2; height)

Use h(A₁ + 4Aₘ + A₂) / 6. Measure the middle area halfway between the parallel ends. This formula is exact when the cross-sectional area varies quadratically or less with height.

Example:

volume_prismoid(9; 16; 25; 3)
= 49

Cylinders, cones and rounded solids

area_cylinder(radius; height)

Use 2π r² + 2π rh for a right circular cylinder, including both ends.

Example:

area_cylinder(2; 3)
= 20pi
area_cylinder_lateral(radius; height)

Use 2π rh for the curved wall.

Example:

area_cylinder_lateral(2; 3)
= 12pi
volume_cylinder(radius; height)

Use π r² h.

Example:

volume_cylinder(10[cm]; 20[cm]) -> [L]
= 2pi L
volume_cylindrical_segment(radius; length; depth)

Multiply the circular segment area by the cylinder length. For a horizontal cylinder with flat ends, depth is the liquid height above the inside bottom. Its range is zero to the diameter.

Example:

volume_cylindrical_segment(2; 4; 2)
= 8pi
area_cone(radius; height)

Add the circular base and curved wall of a right circular cone.

Example:

area_cone(3; 4)
= 24pi
area_cone_lateral(radius; height)

Use π r√(r² + h²) for the curved wall.

Example:

area_cone_lateral(3; 4)
= 15pi
volume_cone(radius; height)

Use π r² h / 3.

Example:

volume_cone(3; 4)
= 12pi
area_cone_frustum(radius_1; radius_2; height)

Add the two circular ends and the curved wall of a right circular cone frustum. A radius of zero gives the tip.

Example:

area_cone_frustum(3; 0; 4)
= 24pi
area_cone_frustum_lateral(radius_1; radius_2; height)

Use π(r₁ + r₂)√(h² + (r₁ − r₂)²) for the curved wall. At least one radius must be positive.

Example:

area_cone_frustum_lateral(3; 3; 4)
= 24pi
volume_cone_frustum(radius_1; radius_2; height)

Use π h(r₁² + r₁r₂ + r₂²) / 3. Enter the two radii in either order.

Example:

volume_cone_frustum(3; 2; 3)
= 19pi
area_hollow_cylinder(outer_radius; inner_radius; height)

Add the inner wall, the outer wall, and both ring-shaped ends. The inner radius must be smaller than the outer radius.

Example:

area_hollow_cylinder(3; 2; 4)
= 50pi
area_hollow_cylinder_lateral(outer_radius; inner_radius; height)

Use 2π(R + r)h for the inner and outer walls.

Example:

area_hollow_cylinder_lateral(3; 2; 4)
= 40pi
volume_hollow_cylinder(outer_radius; inner_radius; height)

Multiply the area of the ring-shaped cross section by the height to get the material volume.

Example:

volume_hollow_cylinder(3; 2; 4)
= 20pi

volume_sphere(3)

area_hemisphere(radius)

Example:

area_hemisphere_lateral(radius)

area_spherical_cap(radius; height)

Add the curved face and the flat disk of area π h(2r − h). Measure height from the cut plane to the tip. It can range from zero to the diameter.

Example:

area_spherical_cap(2; 2)
= 12pi
area_spherical_cap_lateral(radius; height)

Use 2π rh for the curved face.

Example:

area_spherical_cap_lateral(2; 2)

volume_spherical_cap(radius; height)

Use π h²(r − h / 3), with height measured from the cut plane to the tip.

Example:

volume_spherical_cap(2; 1)

area_spherical_segment(radius; lower_z; upper_z)

Add the curved surface and the two cut disks. Enter the signed positions of the lower and upper planes relative to the sphere’s center, in ascending order and within the sphere.

Example:

area_spherical_segment(2; -1; 1)
= 14pi
area_spherical_segment_lateral(radius; lower_z; upper_z)

Example:

area_spherical_shell(outer_radius; inner_radius)

Add the areas of the inner and outer spheres. The inner radius must be smaller than the outer radius.

Example:

volume_spherical_shell(outer_radius; inner_radius)

volume_torus(3; 1)

volume_ellipsoid(semi_axis_1; semi_axis_2; semi_axis_3)

volume_ellipsoid(3; 2; 1)

area_spheroid(equatorial_radius; polar_radius)

Polyhedra and meshes

area_regular_tetrahedron(edge)

Add the four equilateral triangles to get √3s².

Example:

area_regular_tetrahedron(2)
= 4sqrt(3)
volume_regular_tetrahedron(edge)

Use √2s³ / 12.

Example:

volume_regular_tetrahedron(2)
= 2sqrt(2)/3
area_regular_octahedron(edge)

Add the eight equilateral triangles to get 2√3s².

Example:

area_regular_octahedron(2)
= 8sqrt(3)
volume_regular_octahedron(edge)

Use √2s³ / 3.

Example:

volume_regular_octahedron(2)
= 8sqrt(2)/3
area_regular_dodecahedron(edge)

Add the twelve regular pentagons to get 3√(25 + 10√5)s².

Example:

area_regular_dodecahedron(1)
= about 20.645729
volume_regular_dodecahedron(edge)

Use (15 + 7√5)s³ / 4.

Example:

volume_regular_dodecahedron(1)
= 7.66311896062463196872
= (15 + 7 · √(5)) / 4
area_regular_icosahedron(edge)

Add the twenty equilateral triangles to get 5√3s².

Example:

area_regular_icosahedron(2)
= 20sqrt(3)
volume_regular_icosahedron(edge)

Use 5(3 + √5)s³ / 12.

Example:

volume_regular_icosahedron(1)
= about 2.181695
area_tetrahedron(vertices[; scale])

Enter four {x; y; z} rows for the vertices. Add the areas of the four triangular faces. Each face area is half the length of the cross product of two edges.

Example:

area_tetrahedron({{0; 0; 0}; {1; 0; 0}; {0; 1; 0}; {0; 0; 1}})
= 2.36602540378443864676
= (3 + √(3)) / 2
volume_tetrahedron(vertices[; scale])

Use one sixth of the absolute scalar triple product of three edges from the same vertex.

Example:

volume_tetrahedron({{0; 0; 0}; {1; 0; 0}; {0; 1; 0}; {0; 0; 1}})
= 1/6
area_parallelepiped(edges[; scale])

Provide three edge vectors from one corner, one per row. Sum the lengths of the three pairwise cross products and double the result.

Example:

area_parallelepiped({{4; 0; 0}; {0; 3; 0}; {0; 0; 2}})
= 52
volume_parallelepiped(edges[; scale])

Use the absolute scalar triple product of three edge vectors from one corner.

Example:

volume_parallelepiped({{4; 0; 0}; {0; 3; 0}; {0; 0; 2}})
= 24
area_mesh(vertices; faces[; scale])

Sum the areas of the listed triangular faces. Give the vertices as rows of three coordinates and the faces as rows of three vertex numbers, starting at 1. The surface can be open.

Example:

area_mesh({{0; 0; 0}; {1; 0; 0}; {0; 1; 0}; {0; 0; 1}}; {{1; 2; 3}})
= 0.5
volume_mesh(vertices; faces[; scale])

Use the absolute sum of signed tetrahedron volumes, one per face. Faces must form one closed shell with consistent winding and no crossing faces. Each edge must occur twice, in opposite directions.

Example:

volume_mesh({{0; 0; 0}; {1; 0; 0}; {0; 1; 0}; {0; 0; 1}}; {{1; 3; 2}; {1; 2; 4}; {1; 4; 3}; {2; 3; 4}})
= 1/6
